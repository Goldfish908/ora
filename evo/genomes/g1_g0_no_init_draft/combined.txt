[KNOWN PITFALLS — read before writing code]
These are environment/API conventions for this PySCIPOpt harness, distilled
from past failures. Follow them strictly:

1. The prelude already loads `data` from data.json. NEVER reassign `data`
   (e.g. `data = None` or `data = {}` kills every subsequent data[...] access),
   and read data ONLY from data.json — opening any other filename
   (scenario.json, <name>.json, ...) raises FileNotFoundError.
2. m.setObjective: the sense must be the string "minimize" or "maximize"
   (never "min"/"max"), passed as a keyword: m.setObjective(expr, "minimize").
3. setObjective takes ONE expression, not a Python list — sum terms with
   quicksum([...]) first, and never pass a list indirectly via a variable
   (obj = [...]; m.setObjective(obj) also fails).
4. quicksum is a module-level function: from pyscipopt import quicksum.
   m.quicksum(...) and m.sum(...) do not exist.
5. Build comparisons ONLY inside m.addCons(...); never use <=, >=, == on
   expressions in if/assert/bool contexts (it raises TypeError, not a
   constraint).
6. m.infinity is a METHOD: call m.infinity() — passing it uncalled as ub/lb
   raises TypeError.
7. m.setParam only with real SCIP parameter names (e.g. limits/time,
   limits/gap, presolving/maxrounds); never invent parameter names.
8. The code must parse as Python 3: watch indentation and bracket balance,
   especially in long quicksum expressions.
9. PySCIPOpt has NO gurobipy-style attributes: no .ObjVal/.Status/.X/.Pi/.ub/
   .lb/.getLb()/.getUb(). Use m.getObjVal(), m.getStatus(), m.getVal(v),
   m.chgVarLb(v, x) / m.chgVarUb(v, x) instead.

[MODELING PROCEDURE — follow this order BEFORE writing code]
1. UNDERSTAND: one short paragraph — objective and its sense; the decision
   variables; every constraint; and which data fields activate which
   mechanisms (check moq/pack_size/budget_per_period/waste_limit_pct/
   sub_edges/trans_edges/return_rate/labor values in the data schema — a
   zero/null/empty field means that mechanism is INACTIVE for this instance).
2. FORMALIZE: list each decision variable with its domain — CONTINUOUS,
   INTEGER, or BINARY; decide from physical meaning (units of product may be
   continuous; counts of trucks/workers/setups are INTEGER; yes-no decisions
   are BINARY). Then write every constraint as a one-line math statement with
   its index sets, and the objective as a sum of ALL cost/revenue terms
   mentioned in the problem.
3. VERIFY against the problem text: every constraint from the text has an
   addCons, every cost term appears in the objective, every data key you use
   exists in the DATA SCHEMA. Fix any gap, then output the code.


[SEMANTIC CONVENTIONS — demand, substitution, and the solution dump]
These are the EXACT semantics your dumped solution.json is verified against.
Follow them literally; do not substitute your own conventions.
1. Demand identity: the demand of product p at location l in week t is
   demand_curve[p][t-1] * demand_share[l]. Never reinterpret, rescale, or
   re-derive demand from anything else.
2. Demand balance is an EQUALITY per (p,l,t), not a coverage inequality:
       own_sales[p,l,t] + sub_received[p,l,t] + lost[p,l,t] == demand[p,l,t]
   Selling MORE than demand is physically meaningless: customers who do not
   exist cannot buy. If you write demand as a ">=" coverage constraint, the
   optimizer will oversell to harvest phantom revenue.
3. Substitution orientation follows the dump contract EXACTLY:
   sub["pf|pt|l|t"] = units of pf's DEMAND fulfilled by substitute pt.
   Consequences you must enforce in the model:
   - Demand side (pf): own_sales[pf] + sum_pt sub[pf,pt] + lost[pf] == demand[pf].
   - Supply side (pt): units of pt shipped = own_sales[pt] + sum_pf sub[pf,pt]
     must be debited from pt's inventory (include them in pt's outflow /
     inventory balance). One physical unit can never satisfy two demands —
     counting the same units toward both products' demand is double counting.
4. lost[p,l,t] is the unmet demand of exactly that (p,l,t), nonnegative.
   If the instance forbids lost sales, express that as lost == 0 inside the
   balance equality — never drop the term or let it go negative.
5. Dump completeness: solution.json must record EVERY (p,l,t[,r]) index
   combination for sales, I, lost, Q, W, sub, X — including zeros. A missing
   key is read as 0 by the verifier and silently breaks the balance checks
   of every other product/location/week that interacts with it.
6. Big-M hygiene: linking constraints must use a TIGHT big-M (e.g. the
   production capacity of that product/week). Never use 1e20-style
   constants anywhere — they leak phantom flow through the model.
7. Ensure initial inventory variables are explicitly constrained to zero for all SKUs, DCs, and weeks, and verify that demand balance constraints sum only actual sales and substitutions, excluding any residual initial stock terms.
