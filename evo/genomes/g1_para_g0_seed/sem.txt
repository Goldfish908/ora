[SEMANTIC CONVENTIONS — demand, substitution, and the solution dump]
These define the precise semantic criteria against which your exported solution.json is validated.
Adhere strictly to these definitions; do not replace them with alternative conventions.

1. Demand definition: The demand for item p at site l during week t is calculated as
   demand_curve[p][t-1] multiplied by demand_share[l]. Do not alter, rescale, or
   recalculate demand using any other method or source.

2. Demand equilibrium is a strict EQUALITY for each (p,l,t) tuple, not a coverage lower bound:
       own_sales[p,l,t] + sub_received[p,l,t] + lost[p,l,t] == demand[p,l,t]
   Exceeding demand is physically nonsensical: non-existent customers cannot make purchases. If you model demand as a ">=" coverage limit, the optimizer will sell in excess to capture fictitious revenue.

3. Substitution direction must adhere strictly to the dump contract:
   sub["pf|pt|l|t"] represents the quantity of pf's DEMAND that is satisfied by substitute pt.
   Implications that must be enforced within the model:
   - Demand side (pf): own_sales[pf] + sum_pt sub[pf,pt] + lost[pf] == demand[pf].
   - Supply side (pt): the quantity of pt dispatched = own_sales[pt] + sum_pf sub[pf,pt]
     must be deducted from pt's inventory (incorporate these into pt's outflow /
     inventory balance equation). A single physical unit cannot satisfy two separate demands —
     attributing the same units to the demand of both products constitutes double counting.

4. lost[p,l,t] represents the unsatisfied demand for that specific (p,l,t) instance and must be non-negative.
   If the problem instance prohibits lost sales, enforce this by setting lost == 0 within the
   balance equality — do not omit the term or permit it to become negative.

5. Dump exhaustiveness: solution.json must capture ALL (p,l,t[,r]) index
   combinations for sales, I, lost, Q, W, sub, X — including zero values. An absent
   key is interpreted as 0 by the verifier and silently invalidates the balance checks
   for any other product/location/week that has an interaction with it.

6. Starting inventory: unless the business description explicitly specifies initial
   stock levels, inventory in week 1 for every age bucket is ZERO. Do not initialize
   inventory to facilitate model feasibility or simplification.

7. Big-M discipline: linking constraints must employ a TIGHT big-M value (e.g. the
   production capacity for that specific product/week). Do not use generic 1e20-style
   constants anywhere — they permit fictitious flow to pass through the model.
