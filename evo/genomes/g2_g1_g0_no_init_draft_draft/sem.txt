[SEMANTIC CONVENTIONS — demand, substitution, and the solution dump]
These are the EXACT semantics your dumped solution.json is verified against.
Follow them literally; do not substitute your own conventions.
1. Demand identity: the demand of product p at location l in week t is
   demand_curve[p][t-1] * demand_share[l]. Never reinterpret, rescale, or
   re-derive demand from anything else.
2. Demand balance is an EQUALITY per (p,l,t), not a coverage inequality:
       own_sales[p,l,t] + sub_received[p,l,t] + lost[p,l,t] == demand[p,l,t]
   Selling MORE than demand is physically meaningless: customers who do not
   exist cannot buy. If you write demand as a ">=" coverage constraint, the
   optimizer will oversell to harvest phantom revenue.
3. Substitution orientation follows the dump contract EXACTLY:
   sub["pf|pt|l|t"] = units of pf's DEMAND fulfilled by substitute pt.
   Consequences you must enforce in the model:
   - Demand side (pf): own_sales[pf] + sum_pt sub[pf,pt] + lost[pf] == demand[pf].
   - Supply side (pt): units of pt shipped = own_sales[pt] + sum_pf sub[pf,pt]
     must be debited from pt's inventory (include them in pt's outflow /
     inventory balance). One physical unit can never satisfy two demands —
     counting the same units toward both products' demand is double counting.
4. lost[p,l,t] is the unmet demand of exactly that (p,l,t), nonnegative.
   If the instance forbids lost sales, express that as lost == 0 inside the
   balance equality — never drop the term or let it go negative.
5. Dump completeness: solution.json must record EVERY (p,l,t[,r]) index
   combination for sales, I, lost, Q, W, sub, X — including zeros. A missing
   key is read as 0 by the verifier and silently breaks the balance checks
   of every other product/location/week that interacts with it.
6. Big-M hygiene: linking constraints must use a TIGHT big-M (e.g. the
   production capacity of that product/week). Never use 1e20-style
   constants anywhere — they leak phantom flow through the model.
7. Ensure initial inventory variables are explicitly constrained to zero for all SKUs, DCs, and weeks, and verify that demand balance constraints sum only actual sales and substitutions, excluding any residual initial stock terms.
8. Ensure every demand constraint explicitly sums all sales, substitution losses, and lost sales variables against the demand parameter for each SKU, DC, and period to prevent negative or insufficient balance violations.
