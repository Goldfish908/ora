[SEMANTIC CONVENTIONS — demand, substitution, and the solution dump]
These are the precise semantic rules against which your exported solution.json is validated.
Adhere strictly to these definitions; do not impose your own conventions.
1. Demand definition: For any product p, location l, and week t, the demand quantity is calculated as
   demand_curve[p][t-1] * demand_share[l]. Do not re-calculate, adjust the scale, or infer demand using any other method.
2. Demand balance must be expressed as an equality for each (p,l,t) triplet, rather than a coverage inequality:
       own_sales[p,l,t] + sub_received[p,l,t] + lost[p,l,t] == demand[p,l,t]
   Exceeding demand is physically nonsensical: non-existent customers cannot make purchases. If you model demand as a ">=" coverage limit, the optimizer may oversell to capture fictional revenue.
3. The direction of substitution must strictly adhere to the dump contract:
   sub["pf|pt|l|t"] represents the quantity of pf's demand that is satisfied by substitute pt.
   You must enforce the following implications in your model:
   - Demand side (pf): own_sales[pf] + sum_pt sub[pf,pt] + lost[pf] == demand[pf].
   - Supply side (pt): The total units of pt dispatched (equal to own_sales[pt] + sum_pf sub[pf,pt])
     must be deducted from pt's inventory (incorporate this into pt's outflow /
     inventory balance). A single physical unit cannot fulfill two separate demands —
     attributing the same units to both products' demand constitutes double counting.
4. lost[p,l,t] represents the unsatisfied demand for that specific (p,l,t) instance and must be non-negative.
   If the problem instance prohibits lost sales, model this by setting lost == 0 within the
   balance equality — do not remove the variable or allow it to become negative.
5. Completeness of the dump: solution.json must capture every index
   combination (p,l,t[,r]) for sales, I, lost, Q, W, sub, and X — including values of zero. A missing
   key is interpreted as 0 by the verifier, which silently invalidates the balance checks
   for every other product/location/week that interacts with it.
6. Starting inventory: Unless the problem statement explicitly provides initial
   stock levels, the inventory for week 1 across all age buckets must be ZERO. Do not set
   initial inventory to non-zero values to simplify the model or ensure feasibility.
7. Big-M usage: Constraints linking variables must employ a TIGHT big-M (e.g.,
   the production capacity of the specific product in that week). Avoid using 1e20-style
   constants anywhere — they introduce phantom flow into the model.
8. For each SKU and DC, explicitly initialize the inventory variable I[SKU,DC,1,t] to zero for all time periods t; do not assign non-zero starting inventory values or permit unbounded initial stock, given that the problem definition strictly mandates zero initial inventory.
9. Verify demand balance constraints by confirming that the total of sales and substitution adjustments precisely equals the required demand for every SKU-DC-week combination, and set inventory variables to zero unless explicitly stated, thereby preventing infeasibility caused by arithmetic discrepancies or non-zero starting conditions.
